My start of a journey.
2 years and 9 months since i have last programmed.
I've started this project on 06/24/26 at around 15:20 and finished it today on 07/02/26 at around 18:40, 8 days in total.
I learned and re-learned lots of things.

I re-learned
- The purpose of using time.deltaTime when moving something with a transform.
- Properties variables and their importance.
- Events and their importance.
- The need to set a UI to scale with screen size.
- Enums, ifs, fors, switchs, classes, methods, basic things.
- Ternary conditional operators.
- Making menus and layouts and UI things.
- Genericity in code is good most of the time (But not everything needs to be generic or re-usable).
- The importance of debugging and using the assistance of tools for help to find how/where a bug is happening.

I learned
- Why people use m_ at var names and why its good to do it.
- Coding etiquetes (still learning).
- A line only ends at ; making it possible to do better readable formats for things (like ifs, ternarys, etc).
- Object pooling (Not Unity's).
- The importance of decoupling. (But that some cases are ok if it makes sense.)
- Sometimes a single mechanic can end up in the making of lots of mechanics. (A seemingly small scope increase might need lots of other small scope increases)
- How to save things in player pref. (Its mostly good for small things like settings, using it as a save system is not recommended.)
- The useful skill of finishing a project, that yes, i could keep adding and adding but sometimes its better to let go.
- Doing is more important than not doing, in the worst case you can fix it later. (Still its good to try and always maintain good coding etiquette to make maintanance of something manageable in the future. But i shouldn't obsess with "perfect code".)
- One of the most important things in coding is readability and optimization. (But if its something that won't affect performance, sometimes its ok as long its readable.)
- Programmers search a lot and thats ok, even basic stuff. A good programmer is not one that has everything memorized but one that can creatively find a way to do good code. (Which yes, memory helps but that comes naturally.)
- I likely won't come up with the best solution on the first attempt.

Next time i should
- Step back and reevaluate things.
- Do more decoupling.
- Use less public stuff.
- Do better coding.
- Do less over-engineering.
- Search even more.

Eu aprendi e re-aprendi varias coisas.

Eu re-aprendi
- O proposito de usar time.deltaTime quando movendo algo com o transform.
- Variaveis com propriedade e a importancia delas.
- Eventos e sua importancia.
- A necessidade de definir a escala de UI para o tamanho da tela.
- Enums, ifs, fors, switchs, classes, methods, coisas basicas.

- Fazendo menu e layouts e coisas da UI.

Eu aprendi
- O porque pessoas usam m_ nos nomes de variaveis e o porque é bom fazer isso.
- Etiquetas de codigo (Ainda aprendendo).
- A linha apenas acaba no ; fazendo com que formatos mais legiveis paras coisas (tipo ifs, ternarios, etc) sejam possiveis.
- Armazenamento de objetos (object pooling. Não da unity).
- A importancia de desacoplar. (Mas é okay em algums casos se faz sentido)
- As vezes uma unica mecanica pode acabar produzindo muitas mais mecanicas. (Um aparentemente pequeno aumento no escopo do projeto pode necessitar de varios outros pequenos aumentos no escopo.)

- A util abilidade de terminar projetos, na qual sim, eu poderia ficar adicionando e adicionando mas as vezes é melhor só deixar.

- Uma das coisas mais importantes em codigos é leiturabilidade e otimização (Mas se é algo que não afeta performance, as vezes é ok deis de que seja compreensivel).
- Programadores pesquisam bastante e isso é ok, até coisas basicas. Um bom programador não é um que tem tudo memorizado mas sim um que consegue criativamente achar um jeito de fazer um bom codigo. (Na qual sim, memoria ajuda mas isso vem naturalmente)
